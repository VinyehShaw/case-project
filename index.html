TL;DR We explore how a compact 8B vision–language agent can learn to seek longitudinal clinical evidence — deciding which visit, organ, MRI sequence, slice and tool to inspect — and introduce a longitudinal multimodal benchmark built on UK Biobank to evaluate it.

01Abstract

Foundation models can serve as clinical agents through tool-use harnesses. However, conventional medical benchmarks assess reasoning over preselected evidence rather than the ability to seek it across clinical records and longitudinal imaging. We propose CASE: a series of role-specific Clinical Agents for Seeking Evidence, together with a tool-use harness and an agentic post-training framework for compact vision–language policy models. We further introduce a longitudinal multimodal benchmark built on UK Biobank, comprising 50,401 clinical questions derived from real-world ICD-10-coded diagnoses of 4,739 participants. Each question links to a patient-specific environment containing clinical context and multi-sequence MRI from baseline and follow-up visits, where agents autonomously select which visits, organs, modalities, slices, and specialist tools to inspect and compare. Supervised fine-tuning transfers evidence-seeking workflows from 147,434 frontier-model interaction trajectories, followed by agentic reinforcement learning on the learner's own environment interactions. Privileged on-policy self-distillation and rubric-based LLM feedback refine evidence-to-conclusion reasoning without prescribing tool sequences. Experiments show that CASE moves beyond question–answer imitation toward transferable investigation policies, strengthening evidence-grounded longitudinal reasoning. Under matched evaluation conditions, our Qwen3-VL-8B based agent achieves over 16% and 10% relative improvements in answer accuracy over GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.8.

02Given vs. gathered evidence

Conventional medical benchmarks hand the model preselected evidence and ask for an answer. In real longitudinal care, the evidence must be found: across clinical records, visits, organs, MRI sequences and slices. CASE evaluates whether an agent can decide what patient evidence to inspect before it concludes.

Questions are derived from doctor-diagnosed conditions and ICD-10 records in UK Biobank. Each recorded target is paired with cohort-derived distractors, spanning 7 clinical families and 10 task–format strata in three formats: binary, single-select and multi-select.

Every question links to a patient environment with clinical context and baseline plus follow-up MRI across 5 organ regions (heart, brain, pancreas, liver, whole body) and 7 sub-modalities. Tools expose MRI views, Vista3D-derived measurements and overlays, and MedGemma-27B reports.

The metric is first-pass exact answer-set accuracy, reported as Micro (all questions weighted equally) and Macro (all 10 strata weighted equally).

Benchmark and training data are under UK Biobank review. Data are returned to UK Biobank and accessible to approved researchers via UKB-RAP.

06Method

Stage 1

RSFT — rejection-sampled fine-tuning

  • Qwen3.8-Max records role-conditioned trajectories, with reference answers withheld.
  • Kimi K2.6 scores evidence linkage; trajectories with ≥ 1 tool call and score ≥ 0.4 are kept.
  • Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is fine-tuned as a full model, including the vision encoder, with loss on assistant tokens only.

Stage 2

Agentic RL — GRPO on own interactions

  • GRPO with G = 8 rollouts per prompt on the policy's own tool interactions.
  • Tools replay precomputed observations; the policy still chooses tool, arguments, order and stopping.
  • Outcome reward is refined with RLAIF rubric feedback and OPSD.

Trajectory reward

L_j = b_j (0.5 c_j − 0.3 (1 − c_j)) · B_j = r^ans_j + 0.01 r^fmt_j + 0.6 s_j + L_j · R_{n,j} = sg[ B_j − 2 D^CKL_{n,j} ]
r^ans
+1 exact, −1 wrong, −2 unparsed.
s_j
Kimi K2.6 rubric score of the Phase-4 insight: evidence specificity, logical progression, answer support, completeness.
b_j
Indicates a top-2 of 8 insights in the group. Linkage rewards a strong insight only when the answer is correct and penalizes it otherwise.
D^CKL
Privileged self-distillation causal-KL penalty (OPSD), detached and applied before group normalization.

OPSD

Privileged on-policy self-distillation

The same frozen snapshot scores the trajectory under an ordinary view and an answer-privileged view. The reverse KL over the Phase-4 region (top-128 tokens plus one residual bucket) becomes a detached, trajectory-level penalty applied before group normalization. It reorders whole trajectories by synthesis quality without prescribing tool sequences.

Why: in mid-to-late RL the policy stalls in Phase 4, restating findings instead of committing. OPSD acts as a brake on this repetition.

RLAIF

Rubric feedback with top-2 linkage

Kimi K2.6 grades each Phase-4 insight on a rubric. Among the 8 rollouts of a group, the top-2 insights are linked to correctness: a strong insight is rewarded only when its answer is correct, and penalized otherwise, so persuasive but wrong synthesis is not reinforced.

Each component adds on top of GRPO. RLAIF lifts insight quality the most on its own, while OPSD contributes the larger accuracy gain; combining both gives the best accuracy and insight score.

Late collapse without OPSD. In mid-to-late RL the policy tends to stall in Phase 4, restating findings instead of committing to an answer. Without the OPSD brake, GRPO+RLAIF's correctness reward collapses late in training (shaded band in Figure 3), whereas CASE-8B keeps improving.
